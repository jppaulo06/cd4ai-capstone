\chapter{Case Studies}
\label{chap:case-studies}

This chapter examines how CD4AI's responsibilities appear in Divination and in
the documented workflows of DeepEval with Confident AI, Braintrust, and MLflow.
The analysis considers how observations become reusable cases, how those cases
are evaluated, and how the results support decisions. The evidence varies:
Divination provides an inspectable implementation, while the other cases provide
documented workflows and supporting capabilities.

\section{How to Read the Cases}
\label{sec:cases-overview}

Each case identifies its context and evidence, follows the connections between
monitoring, curation, and evaluation, and examines result interpretation and
delivery policy. This mapping uses the responsibilities defined in
Chapter~\ref{chap:cd4ai}; it does not imply that the external projects adopted
the CD4AI name.

The documentary evidence includes source snapshots captured on September 21,
2026. Braintrust's comparison and CI documentation was additionally captured on
October 31, 2026. These snapshots preserve the reviewed material independently
of later changes to online documentation. Divination is examined at commit
\texttt{44e6194}. The case repository records source locations, capture limits,
and the provenance of its illustrations.

Documentation can establish a recommended workflow or an available mechanism.
Source inspection can establish how a mechanism is implemented. Neither alone
establishes that a team completed the cycle or achieved a quality improvement.
The external platforms were not operated for this study. Divination's synthetic
interface examples also do not constitute an observed deployment experiment.

\subsection{Evaluation Results and Delivery Decisions}
\label{sec:cases-evaluation-purpose}

Evidence for the feedback loop is considered separately from evidence for
interpreting results and applying a delivery policy. In particular, the analysis
asks what supports a distinction between continuing limitations and deterioration
in established behavior, and how that distinction affects development or
delivery. It does not assume that cases occupy fixed required and optional groups.

A documented assertion or failed CI job establishes a mechanism for rejecting
an evaluation result. Whether that result prevents integration or deployment
also depends on the surrounding policy and its enforcement. Similarly, support
for comparison or metadata does not establish how a team manages known issues.
When the reviewed sources leave these matters unspecified, the case identifies
an evidence gap rather than inferring that the behavior is present or absent.

\section{Divination}
\label{sec:case-divination}

\subsection{Context and Evidence}

Divination is a conversational assistant that uses retrieval-augmented generation
to answer questions about Dungeons \& Dragons. The inspected revision includes
evaluation suites, interaction storage, candidate signals, and human review.
The evidence consists of versioned source and project documentation, including
the monitoring, evaluation, and CI guides~\parencite{Divination:Monitoring,
Divination:Evaluations,Divination:CI}. The existing interface illustrations use
synthetic data and an English interface adaptation; they illustrate the tools
without demonstrating an executed evaluation cycle.

\subsection{Monitoring, Curation, and Evaluation}

Monitoring retains questions, answers, and retrieved passages. Detectors flag
potential problems, and negative user feedback also generates a signal. Human
review can examine interactions with and without signals, recording verdicts
and rationales. This separates candidate selection from the judgment that a
problem occurred~\parencite{Divination:Monitoring}.

The connection from review to evaluation is manual. The evaluation guide
instructs a developer to inspect a confirmed defect, determine expected behavior,
and add a case to the versioned dataset. The case specifies an opening question,
a scenario, and an expected outcome. Single-turn and simulated conversational
evaluations consume this dataset. A review record alone therefore does not add
an executable case to the suite~\parencite{Divination:Evaluations}.

\subsection{Result Interpretation and Delivery Decisions}

The documented procedure exercises a new case before and after a fix and then
commits the case with the fix. It describes a path from confirmation to later
regression protection. It leaves unspecified how unresolved failing cases enter
the active suite and how their failures are distinguished from newly introduced
problems~\parencite{Divination:Evaluations}.

Single-turn evaluations run in the pull-request workflow, and the CI runner
propagates subprocess failures. However, the evaluation commands permit errors
to be ignored and assessments with missing parameters to be skipped. The CI guide
explicitly states that successful execution does not guarantee that every metric
was evaluated~\parencite{Divination:CI}. Repository enforcement and an actual
deployment gate were not verified in this analysis.

\subsection{Connections and Limitations}

Divination supplies inspectable implementations of the stages and instructions
for the manual dataset handoff. The reviewed evidence does not follow one
application failure through curation, a confirmed fix, and a later delivery
decision. Demonstrating that sequence would strengthen the case. An explicit
policy for unresolved cases and incomplete assessments would also make the
relationship between evaluation results and acceptance clearer.

\section{DeepEval and Confident AI}
\label{sec:case-deepeval}

\subsection{Context and Evidence}

This case examines the combined workflow of the DeepEval evaluation framework
and the Confident AI platform. Its evidence consists of archived guides covering
agent evaluation, conversational evaluation, annotation queues, and dataset
management~\parencite{DeepEval:AgentGuide,DeepEval:MultiTurn,
ConfidentAI:AnnotationQueues,ConfidentAI:Datasets}. The analysis concerns the
workflow those sources describe; no application or platform account was used
to execute it during this review.

\subsection{Monitoring, Curation, and Evaluation}

The conversational guide connects production monitoring to development datasets:
failing conversations can become retained scenarios for later evaluation.
Production conversations are collected and assessed, while development runs
exercise scenarios and inspect their results~\parencite{DeepEval:MultiTurn}.

Curation appears in the preparation of the retained examples, called
\emph{goldens}. Annotation queues organize review, and dataset management supports
expected outputs, contextual information, and review before finalization.
These activities provide a connection between an observed interaction and the
expectations used in future assessments~\parencite{ConfidentAI:AnnotationQueues,
ConfidentAI:Datasets}. Finalizing a golden establishes the readiness of evaluation
data; it does not demonstrate that the application satisfies the case.

\subsection{Result Interpretation and Delivery Decisions}

The agent guide describes metric thresholds and assertions that fail the build
when evaluation criteria are not met~\parencite{DeepEval:AgentGuide}. This supports
an implementation in which selected checks must pass. A team still needs to
connect the resulting status to its integration or deployment policy.

The inspected guides do not specify how unresolved failing cases coexist with
those assertions or how a report distinguishes continuing problems from new
deterioration. A threshold determines whether an outcome meets a criterion;
additional evidence is needed to determine whether a change introduced the
problem. Available metadata could support such interpretation, but its presence
does not demonstrate that a particular team uses it for that purpose.

\subsection{Connections and Limitations}

The documented return of production failures to development datasets supports
the feedback loop. Dataset review and CI assertions provide concrete mechanisms
for curation and evaluation. The evidence does not establish a team's complete
process for tracking known limitations, confirming fixes, or changing a case's
role after improvement. Those responsibilities remain unverified in the reviewed
workflow, rather than demonstrated by the framework's installation alone.

\section{Braintrust}
\label{sec:case-braintrust}

\subsection{Context and Evidence}

Braintrust's documented workflow connects instrumentation, observation,
annotation, evaluation, and deployment~\parencite{Braintrust:Workflow}.
The case uses the archived workflow, human-review, and dataset guides, supplemented
by the October comparison and CI snapshots. Published interface examples show
separate activities, not successive steps of one interaction observed in this
study.

\subsection{Monitoring, Curation, and Evaluation}

Logs provide material for investigating application behavior. Reviewers can
record judgments and expected values, and selected traces can be added to
datasets. Experiments then assess changes on those retained examples
\parencite{Braintrust:HumanReview,Braintrust:Datasets,Braintrust:Workflow}.
The annotation stage makes preparation for evaluation explicit. For a defective
answer, the retained expectation must describe the desired behavior; copying
the observed answer alone would not establish the criterion for a correction.

\subsection{Result Interpretation and Delivery Decisions}

Braintrust supports comparisons against a baseline, exposing score changes for
individual cases and allowing inspection of outputs and repeated trials
\parencite{Braintrust:Comparison}. These mechanisms can support investigation
of changes while continuing failures remain visible. Choosing a baseline does
not, by itself, establish that its behavior was accepted. The team must also
consider whether differences reflect application changes or assessment variability.

The CI guide describes pull-request reporting and customizable reporters that
control the process outcome. Its default exception handling is distinct from a
team's quality acceptance rule~\parencite{Braintrust:CI}. A report of deteriorating
scores therefore needs a policy defining which results prevent a change from
proceeding and how that decision is enforced.

\subsection{Connections and Limitations}

This case supports the documented feedback loop and provides mechanisms relevant
to the interpretation discussed in Section~\ref{sec:cd4ai-result-interpretation}.
Its comparison features make it possible to investigate changes without a
mandatory division into separate datasets. The reviewed sources do not establish
a particular team's policy for unresolved limitations, its choice of accepted
reference behavior, or a case followed through a confirmed fix. The mapping
therefore concerns documented responsibilities and configurable mechanisms,
with those operational decisions left unverified.

\section{MLflow}
\label{sec:case-mlflow}

\subsection{Context and Evidence}

The MLflow case examines archived documentation for regression testing, review
queues, and evaluation datasets~\parencite{MLflow:RegressionGuide,
MLflow:ReviewQueues,MLflow:Datasets}. These sources describe how captured behavior
can inform subsequent checks. No MLflow server or evaluation was run for this
review, and the published interface examples do not represent a cycle executed
for the study.

\subsection{Monitoring, Curation, and Evaluation}

The documented loop begins with traces and feedback, identifies a failure, and
turns it into an executable check with expected behavior. Review queues organize
inspection and retain judgments or expectations. Traces can also become dataset
records for later evaluations~\parencite{MLflow:RegressionGuide,
MLflow:ReviewQueues,MLflow:Datasets}. Review and case preparation supply the
curation responsibility; collecting traces alone would leave that connection
incomplete.

\subsection{Result Interpretation and Delivery Decisions}

The regression-testing guide distinguishes measuring performance across a dataset
from enforcing particular behaviors through pass/fail assertions. In its example,
every scorer must pass for every row; a failed assertion fails the CI job
\parencite{MLflow:RegressionGuide}. Making that check mandatory for integration
requires the corresponding repository policy.

The dataset guide identifies assessing fixes on problematic production examples,
preserving behavior, and comparing versions as uses of evaluation
\parencite{MLflow:Datasets}. These purposes fit the broader evaluation stage in
CD4AI. The distinction between measurement and gating does not establish that
a team necessarily stores their cases in two separate datasets.

\subsection{Connections and Limitations}

The sources provide a documented path from captured failures to subsequent checks,
including an explicit assertion-based decision mechanism. They do not specify
how temporarily permitted failures are tracked or distinguished from new
regressions. Although the guide calls the retained checks regression tests,
creating a check from a failure does not establish that the expected behavior
has already been achieved. Its protective purpose must be interpreted alongside
the application's history, as discussed in Chapter~\ref{chap:cd4ai}.
